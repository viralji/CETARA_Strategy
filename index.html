The big insight from this timeline

The 2019 crisis was the trigger, not the cause. Savvy and Indane gained share in the same downturn. WWT's real problem was structural — capex without payback, weak NPS, and lagging digital readiness — which had been building quietly since 2016.

Key point: "WWT cannot blame the economy. Rivals thrived in the same conditions. The decline is self-inflicted, which means it is also self-correctable."

WWT — Strategic SWOT

Strengths

  • Pioneer DNA — string of "firsts" (fixed-line, undersea cable, NLD/ILD)
  • 32,900 km fiber backbone + undersea cable to Singapore
  • Strong "Systel Ind" brand recall
  • Mobile cash cow (63% of revenue, +46.75% growth)
  • $17B market cap; Savvy-backed credibility
  • Diversified portfolio: Mobile + B&T + Enterprise

Weaknesses

  • Capex-heavy with weakening payback (+124% capex / −58% EBIT in B&T)
  • Poor NPS, especially in South-East
  • Lagging in AI, digital channels, self-service
  • Only 2.6% fixed-line share — subscale
  • High-ARPU segment saturated
  • Outsourcing without strong governance creating CX leakage

Opportunities

  • 5G fixed wireless access (capex-light reach)
  • IoT (>$600B by 2022) for enterprises
  • AI-led service (Savvy proved 68% CSAT lift is possible)
  • Triple-play bundles for premium homes
  • Enterprise managed services + SDN/NFV
  • DLT-enabled payments / fintech adjacencies
  • Smartphone-led India: 2nd largest market

Threats

  • Government-led price wars (SNL below-cost broadband)
  • OTT players eroding voice / messaging revenue
  • Wi-Fi / wireless substitution of fixed-line
  • Savvy's dual role (investor + rival) — info leak risk
  • Customer expectations resetting via digital-only operators
  • Returns on equity already below cost of capital

Inference — what this picture tells us

  • The problem is not demand — it is profitability and competitiveness. WWT spends more to grow but earns less.
  • It is a multi-front war, not a single-rival fight. Each competitor attacks a different dimension (price, tech, experience, acquisition).
  • WWT's historical strength was agility. It lost ground because competitors became more agile. Strategy = restore agility.
  • Savvy is the trickiest piece. 10% stake + competing → governance and information protection is a real risk, not paranoia.
  • The path forward is not "cheaper". It is "smarter, more premium, more digital".

From legacy telecom to premium digital technology leader

Where WWT has been, and where it must go. This is not just a strategy shift — it is a worldview shift.

History — Who we were (2009–2018)

  • Fixed-line and copper-cable thinking
  • Product-led: sell connections, plans, lines
  • Call-center reactive support
  • Capex-heavy blanket expansion
  • Defensive competition (match every offer)
  • Organic-only growth
  • Scattered projects, siloed BUs
  • Brand built on "firsts" — but resting on past glory
→

Manifestation — Who we will become (2025+)

  • Access-agnostic connectivity (fiber + 5G FWA + Wi-Fi)
  • Relationship-led: own the customer for life
  • AI-led proactive service (predict, prevent, personalize)
  • Smart capital — capex where payback is clear
  • Selective battlefield strategy (pick where to fight)
  • Partnerships + M&A + ecosystem plays
  • One unified transformation agenda
  • Brand built on premium digital trust — earning today

Why now? The motivation

WWT has three forces converging that make this the right moment, not a hopeful one:

  1. The pain is undeniable. RMS down 448 bps, EBIT down 58.9%, NPS poor — leadership has the burning platform to act.
  2. The market is still growing. Telecom heading to $3.45T by 2022 at 6.4% CAGR. IoT > $600B. The pie is expanding — we just need to claim our slice differently.
  3. The window will close. Savvy is already ahead on AI. TelZip is locking customers into long-term contracts. Every quarter we wait, recovery gets more expensive.
WWT became successful because it was agile and innovative. It is losing because competitors became more agile than WWT. The strategy is not reinvention — it is restoration of market agility.

The promise we make to ourselves

We will not be the cheapest. We will be the most trusted.

We will not chase every customer. We will defend every customer worth keeping. We will not invest everywhere. We will invest where it compounds. We will not depend on any single partner — including the one that owns 10% of us. We will be lighter, sharper, and harder to attack 12 months from now.

Project 1 of 5 · Hypothalamus

WWT Stars Shine

Theme: Customer retention · Premium experience · Emotional connection · Service excellence

Core idea

Stop losing valuable customers to neglect. Turn premium and ultra-premium customers into brand advocates, not just retained accounts.

Strategic objective

Become the #1 telecom for customer experience among premium and ultra-premium customers in India.

Why this matters (the case evidence)

The case data is unambiguous: 48% of switchers cite "better service quality" and 23% cite "personalized experience" as the reason they leave. NPS in the South-East is "Poor/OK" — and 32% of low-NPS customers plan to switch within a year (vs only 14% of high-NPS). Customer experience is not soft. It is direct revenue protection.

Key initiatives

  1. Zero valuable-customer loss program — a war room for premium / ultra-premium / SME / enterprise. Every at-risk customer has an owner.
  2. Premium customer command center — real-time dashboard: churn risk, complaint aging, NPS, outages, install delays, repeat complaints, competitor offers, escalations.
  3. AI-led service recovery — predict churn, fault, dispute resolution; trigger retention offers proactively.
  4. White-glove service layer — priority install & repair, dedicated lines, outage alerts, home network checks, premium router upgrades, annual relationship review.
  5. Emotional brand campaign — "Connected Across Generations" — landline + broadband as the trusted backbone of Indian families across decades.
  6. Landline-to-digital transition — landline + broadband + Wi-Fi + IPTV/OTT + digital payments + elderly support + home IoT/security.

Out-of-the-box ideas

  • WWT Family Legacy Plan — reward multi-year families
  • Grandparents Digital Care Pack — broadband + video calling + fraud-safe payments + priority support
  • Never Offline Promise — backup connectivity or service credits for premium
  • Customer Apology Protocol — senior leader calls after repeated failures
  • One Engineer, One Relationship — field engineers assigned to premium neighborhoods
  • "My WWT" companion app — outage map, billing, plan tweaks, repair status

Timeline

Days 0–30 · AlignIdentify top churn-risk premium customers. Stand up command center MVP. Map South-East as priority market 1.
Days 31–60 · PilotLaunch Stars Shine in South-East. AI chatbot pilot. First retention squad operational. White-glove pilot in 3 cities.
Days 61–90 · ScaleScale retention plays. Launch "Connected Across Generations" campaign. Family Legacy Plan rollout.

Success factors

  • CEO-level visibility on top 100 customer issues, weekly
  • NPS as a board-reported metric, quarterly
  • Field empowerment to resolve at first contact
  • Partner SLAs tied to CX outcomes, not transaction counts
  • Sales + service + technical teams sharing one customer record

Fallback plan (if NPS gains are slow)

  • Targeted win-back offers for churned high-ARPU customers
  • Selective tariff matching only in defended cities
  • Bring partial customer service back in-house for top 5% accounts
  • Acquire a regional CX-strong broadband ISP for talent + base
  • Increase service credits / SLA-backed contracts as confidence proof

Jump 5 Steps

Theme: Technology leadership · 5G · IoT · AI · Future readiness

Core idea

WWT cannot catch up by walking — it must leapfrog in carefully chosen technology areas. Don't invest everywhere; jump where leadership is reclaimable.

Strategic objective

Restore WWT's "first mover" identity by leading in 5G-enabled, IoT-enabled, AI-powered digital connectivity for premium & enterprise India.

Key initiatives

  1. Technology battlefield selection — focus only where leadership is reclaimable: 5G FWA, IoT for enterprise, AI customer service, predictive network maintenance, premium home broadband, SME digital services, managed connectivity, SDN/NFV.
  2. 5G premium pilot zones — high-value micro-markets: residential clusters, business districts, tech parks, hospitals, educational hubs.
  3. IoT enterprise verticals — logistics, smart buildings, energy, industrial, healthcare, education campus, smart retail.
  4. AI-first operations — chatbot support, fault & churn prediction, network optimization, technician routing, billing dispute resolution.
  5. Capex rationalization — rank every market on ARPU, churn risk, competitor intensity, payback. Investment principle: "Fiber where density pays, fixed wireless where speed matters, partnerships where capital is expensive, digital where service can scale."

  • First Again Challenge — internal contest to reclaim "first in India" status
  • Smart Premium Homes — broadband + IoT security + OTT + cloud backup bundle
  • 5G-in-a-Box for SMEs — plug-and-play SME connectivity
  • Network Twin — digital simulation of network assets to predict failures and capex needs
  • Open RAN partnership — vendor-neutral cost reduction

Fallback plan (if 5G/IoT slips)

LightHouse WWT

Theme: Internal alignment · Partners · People · Technology · Governance · Culture

Core idea

WWT cannot deliver premium CX if its internal organization is slow, siloed, leaky, or misaligned. Remove past baggage. Make the company lighter, faster, more accountable.

Strategic objective

Rebuild WWT internally so people, partners, processes, technology, and governance are aligned to premium digital leadership.

Key initiatives

  1. One-company operating model — break silos: mobile, broadband, enterprise, network, customer service, digital, field, sales. Shared targets for CX and profitable growth.
  2. Partner governance reset — outsource tasks, not accountability. Partner scorecards, SLA contracts, CX-linked payouts, repeat-failure penalties, joint reviews.
  3. Employee experience & capability uplift — train on premium handling, digital tools, consultative selling, competitor response, enterprise solutions, data security, empathy.
  4. Information protection program — given Savvy's dual role: review board/investor info rights, classify sensitive data, restrict pricing/capex/churn/strategy access, formal confidentiality protocols.
  5. Agile transformation office — CEO-led; weekly execution dashboard; monthly promoter review; quarterly board review.
  6. Remove past baggage — kill loss-making products, unproductive capex, slow vendor contracts, legacy systems, duplicated teams, non-performing geographies.

Out-of-the-box ideas

  • Kill Complexity Day — monthly: remove one process, report, approval, or legacy product
  • Customer Room — leadership listens to real complaints weekly
  • Reverse Mentoring — young digital employees mentor senior leaders
  • Partner League Table — rank partners publicly inside the company
  • WWT War Room — top 100 customer issues reviewed daily until fixed
  • "Confidential by default" data tagging — for Savvy-facing reports

Timeline

Days 0–30 · AlignTransformation office stood up. 5 project owners appointed. Savvy info-rights review. Top 50 legacy items identified for kill list.
Days 31–60 · PilotPartner SLAs reviewed. Customer Room launches. First Kill Complexity Day. Reverse Mentoring cohort 1.
Days 61–90 · ScalePartner scorecards rolled out. Information protocol approved by board. First transformation scorecard to promoters.

Success factors

  • CEO is the visible transformation leader (not a delegated CTO/COO)
  • Weekly rhythm — no slipping to monthly
  • Board-approved governance for Savvy info flow
  • HR aligned: hire/fire authority for non-performers within 90 days
  • Promoter buy-in on "remove past baggage" mandate before launch

Fallback plan

  • If silos resist — restructure into customer-segment-led P&Ls instead of product-led BUs
  • If outsourcing partners cannot meet new SLAs — insource top 2 functions (premium CX, network ops in SE)
  • If Savvy contests info restrictions — escalate to board; consider stake repurchase
  • If transformation office stalls — bring in external CTO/COO for the 12-month transformation only

Orbit Growth

Theme: Inorganic growth · Partnerships · Acquisitions · Ecosystem expansion

Core idea

Organic growth alone will not restore leadership in time. Use partnerships, selective M&A, and ecosystem plays to accelerate capability and market access — without carrying all the capex.

Strategic objective

Build growth options beyond legacy telecom through acquisitions, partnerships, cross-services, and capital-light models.

Key initiatives

  1. Strategic partnership for 5G capex — infrastructure / tower / fiber sharing, vendor financing, private network partnerships, enterprise co-investment.
  2. Acquire capabilities, not just subscribers — regional broadband ISPs in premium clusters; Wi-Fi hotspot providers; IoT startups; cloud / security MSPs; AI CX platforms; field-service tech; enterprise connectivity specialists.
  3. Cross-service partnerships — OTT, cloud, cybersecurity, payments, smart home, edtech, healthtech, enterprise SaaS.
  4. Premium ecosystem bundles — premium home digital, SME business continuity, smart office, student learning, elderly care, secure digital payments.
  5. M&A screening framework — every target scored on: segment fit, premium fit, tech capability, payback, integration risk, NPS impact, cross-sell potential, strategic control.

Out-of-the-box ideas

  • WWT Digital Club — premium sub: broadband + OTT + cloud + cybersecurity + priority service + smart home
  • Connectivity-as-a-Service for SMEs — monthly: broadband + backup + security + cloud + device support
  • Acquire the customer front door — buy/partner with apps that already own premium relationships
  • Neighborhood fiber clubs — pre-sell premium broadband to communities before capex
  • Enterprise co-build — business parks co-invest in WWT infra for premium service
  • Telecom-led fintech pilot — DLT-enabled low-cost payments adjacency
